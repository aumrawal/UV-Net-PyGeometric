The weak classes are not mixing up through and blind

Through and blind partners make up 7.7% of all wrong faces. The largest group, 27.5%, is features of the same depth that differ in how many sides are open, such as a rectangular passage called a rectangular through slot.

The stronger pattern is where the errors sit. 71% of wrong feature faces belong to features whose face count differs from the usual one for their class, which is what happens when a neighbouring feature cuts into them. Those features hold only 22% of the feature faces.

What this means for the next variant

  • Whether a cut goes through the part is not the main gap. A change aimed only at that would address under a tenth of the errors.
  • Features cut by neighbours are the main gap. A passage that has lost a wall looks like a slot. This analysis cannot tell whether the surrounding faces hold enough information to recover the right label, or whether some of these cases are ambiguous in the data.
  • Round is a local geometry problem, between convex and concave curved faces and chamfers. Edge convexity or face curvature are the candidate inputs. It is 3% of all errors.
  • The split in the last table is now a metric. Any variant can be scored separately on usual and unusual features, which shows what it fixed.
